# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Shaun (designer-engineer) is the primary user: he makes textured image and video assets for his portfolio site, working on a laptop at varied hours. Secondary users are portfolio visitors, mostly designers and design-curious engineers, who land on the tool as a demonstration of craft and stay to play. Both arrive wanting a beautiful result fast; visitors additionally need the tool to teach itself through discovery, not documentation.

## Product Purpose

Shader Lab turns any image, video, solid, or pattern into print-inspired artwork (halftone, dither, ASCII, hatching, risograph-style grades) through a stackable effect pipeline, then exports stills and clips at production quality. It exists to be a robust, genuinely useful asset-making instrument first and a delightful toy second. Success: a first-time visitor reaches a result they love within a minute, and Shaun trusts the exports enough to ship them on his own site.

## Brand Personality

Print shop, not spaceship. Precise, tactile, quietly playful: an instrument with the soul of physical media (newsprint, risograph, notebook dot grids, engraving). Slight skeuomorphic warmth is welcome where it aids intuition; the chrome otherwise disappears so the artwork is the hero.

## Anti-references

- Cream/beige "AI-generated" surface tints; the chrome stays plain neutral.
- Generic dark neon "AI tool" aesthetic; gradient text, glassmorphism, hero metrics.
- Noisy decorative backgrounds (grids, checkerboards as decoration).
- Harsh black-outlined controls on white.
- Preset walls: long undifferentiated chip lists with no visual preview.

## Design Principles

1. The artwork is the color. Chrome is restrained neutral; the shader output provides all vibrancy.
2. Grounded in physical media. Every effect's defaults target the real-world ideal it descends from (Leuchtturm dot grid, CMYK newsprint, engraving hatching), not the mathematical artifact.
3. Show, don't list. Discovery happens through visual previews and curated starting points, never through walls of text options.
4. Great out of the box. Default parameters must look like the reference imagery for that effect with zero tweaking; Advanced is for rare intent.
5. Robust instrument. Exports are trustworthy, state survives reloads, controls behave like a professional tool (hover states, keyboard access, consistent affordances).

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA for the tool chrome (contrast, focus-visible rings, keyboard reachability on all controls including drag-reorder alternatives). Respect prefers-reduced-motion: no decorative animation, transitions convey state only. Color information is never the sole signal (gamut/contrast readouts include text).
